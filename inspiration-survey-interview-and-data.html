Interview and Data

Interviews

In addition to the survey, in-person interviews were conducted with youth participants at the agency in fall 2023 — questions ranged from hobbies and daily routine to what triggers a tantrum, how they try to calm down, and what they hope for in the future.

What the interviews surfaced

Complex triggers: tantrums traced back to a mix of causes — past trauma, instability, adjusting to a new placement, or academic stress — and trusted adults, caregivers, and friends consistently showed up as the biggest factor in how well a child coped. Coping mechanisms varied by kid: art, sports, and specific relaxation techniques all came up. Despite everything, resilience and hope were a throughline — dreams of a forever home, doing well in school, reconnecting with family.

Age-related differences: teenagers tended to articulate feelings more explicitly and lean on more varied coping strategies (friends, creative outlets); younger kids relied on simpler, more physical outlets and had a harder time putting emotions into words. Both groups had experienced trauma, but it showed up differently — teens showed more self-awareness, younger kids more adaptability. A stable, supportive environment came up as important across every age group.

Survey results

Of survey respondents, 45.5% said they never experience violent tantrums, 27.3% said occasionally, and 18.2% reported experiencing them frequently or almost daily. The takeaway wasn't a single number — it was that tantrum frequency varies widely, and support needs to be tailored rather than one-size-fits-all. The ~18% experiencing frequent tantrums represented a group worth specifically designing for.
