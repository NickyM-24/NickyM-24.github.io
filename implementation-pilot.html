Pilot: Preparing to Pilot

How I am getting ready to run my pilot: who is involved, where sessions happen, how families are invited in, and how I will collect data along the way. My pilot runs at A Parentz Touch ABA with two to three caregivers and their children.

  • Site: A Parentz Touch ABA
  • Supervisor: L'Tricia Turner
  • Settings: family homes, the agency, and community locations
  • Data: baseline before any strategy, weekly review, Week 10 midpoint, Week 15 final

The short version

A pilot is not a small version of a finished program. It is a way to learn what actually happens when real people use something, in the conditions they actually live in.

There is no single setting. Sessions happen in family homes, at the agency, and in community locations, because my supervisor asked that children practice regulating in their own natural environments. A strategy that works in a quiet therapy room and fails in a loud public place has not actually been tested.

My audience is two or three families, so my communication plan is a handful of individual conversations rather than a campaign. Recruitment runs through the agency, and the first ask is small: try one strategy, with me there the first time, and tell me afterward whether it was worth their time.

My supervisor asked me to stay level headed and focus on where each family currently is, not where I want them to end up. I write my log after each session rather than during it, and I treat a difficult session as information rather than failure.
