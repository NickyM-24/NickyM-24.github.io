Monitor and Evaluate: Impact and Evaluation

How I will know whether my project is working, and what I will do when it is not. My pilot, Skill Reinforcement through Therapeutic Activities, runs fifteen weeks at A Parentz Touch ABA with two to three caregivers and their children.

  • Project: Skill Reinforcement through Therapeutic Activities
  • Site: A Parentz Touch ABA
  • Supervisor: L'Tricia Turner
  • Measured at: Week 5 baseline, Week 10 midpoint, Week 15 final

The short version

I kept my evaluation to three indicators instead of trying to measure everything. Each one ties back to a specific project goal, and each child and caregiver is compared against their own starting point rather than against each other.

The Week 10 midpoint exists so I can change what is not working while there is still time to act on it. A low or slow moving score tells me something about fit and readiness. It is not a sign of failure, and often the right response is to slow down rather than push harder.

My supervisor's biggest point was that I cannot put this project on families. I have to meet caregivers where they are. That is why my caregiver survey now starts by asking what they actually need, and why it checks whether the work fits into their lives and whether their input is shaping it.
