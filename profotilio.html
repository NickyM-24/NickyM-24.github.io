About Me

I am a student at Arizona State University and an upcoming educator dedicated to improving the lives of people by furthering my career in the education field. I have had a desire to teach children since I was very young. One of my goals in life is to meet every child's needs and desires as if they were my own. I believe the best way to teach people is to involve them in the teaching process — by doing so, we gain a deep understanding of their needs and perspectives, and can create solutions that truly work for them.

My work as an RBT

Alongside my coursework, I work as a Registered Behavior Technician. When I started, I understood the role in fairly practical terms: run the programs, collect the data, follow the behavior plan. What it has become is something considerably deeper.

Working directly with children has changed how I think about what learning actually requires. A child cannot learn in a space that does not meet them where they are. Before any teaching can happen, the environment has to be right — the communication has to be accessible, the sensory demands have to be manageable, the expectations have to match the child in front of me rather than an average child on paper. My job is not to move a child toward a standard. It is to build the conditions in which that particular child can actually learn.

That shift is what connects this work to everything else in this portfolio. The human-centered design process I have used throughout my internship rests on the same principle: you do not design for people, you design with them, starting from their real experience rather than your assumptions about it. Involving a child in their own learning — noticing what sets them off, what settles them, what motivates them — is the same practice applied one person at a time.

Where I am headed

I am working toward becoming a Board Certified Behavior Analyst. Certification as a BCBA would let me move from carrying out behavior plans to designing them — assessing what a child actually needs, building the intervention around that, and training the people who deliver it day to day. It is the same commitment I hold now, with a wider reach: making sure children are learning in the space of their need, not in spite of it.
