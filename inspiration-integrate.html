Revising the prototype

The flier went through a second iteration incorporating the feedback from the previous round — clearer implementation guidance and more accessible language across different therapist and client contexts.

Feedback on the revision

The revised prototype was well received for its trauma-informed, user-friendly design and its consideration of different cultural backgrounds and individual experiences. The survey approach specifically was praised for clarity in capturing both caregiver and child perspectives, and the ethical handling of privacy throughout the research was called out directly.

Process, end to end

Looking back at the full arc: start with caregiver perspectives (interviews/surveys) → build an initial prototype around their feedback → pilot-test with a small group of caregivers → shift focus to the kids' own perspectives, using child-friendly methods → bring in child psychologists for age-appropriate framing → build a child-centric version of the prototype → observe and gather direct feedback from kids → refine → arrive at a final, integrated prototype.

Where this could go next

One direction worth pursuing further: a Trauma-Informed Emotional Support System — extending this work into a more complete environment for kids in foster care dealing with trauma-driven tantrums, built around the same principle of tailoring support to the individual child rather than a one-size-fits-all program.
