Program Outcomes

Education in Society

How education functions within broader social, cultural, and political contexts, and who it serves well.

The domain

The Education in Society domain addresses the functions, values, and impact of education in diverse communities. The main skills addressed through this domain are students' abilities to (1) compare and contrast education across multiple populations and settings; (2) persuasively articulate the connection between the values of a learning setting and the practices and policies within that setting; and (3) participate in informed debate related to fundamental questions about social justice, equity, opportunity, and fairness in education.

Arizona State University, Mary Lou Fulton Teachers College. (n.d.). Overview of Ed Studies program domains [Course document].

The work I chose

  • Course: LSE 401: Designing Learning Environments for Inclusion
  • Work: Universal Design evaluation of Knott's Berry Farm and a business letter proposal to the park

Description

For this assignment, I visited a community space and evaluated it against the seven principles of Universal Design, a framework for making environments usable by all people regardless of ability, age, language, or background. I chose Knott's Berry Farm because I had visited the park with one of my clients and her family.

I observed how an open public space actually worked for families with children on the spectrum, with disabilities, or both. I then wrote a business letter to the park's operations leadership that described what I saw and proposed specific changes, and I sent it.

Representation: how this work shows my mastery

This was a real life experience outside of classroom expectations. Rather than studying inclusion in the abstract, I watched how a public space worked for the families who use it, and it changed how I think about where education happens and who is responsible for access.

OutcomeHow this work shows it
ES.A Compare Various Outcomes of EducationA theme park is not a school, but it teaches constantly, and what it teaches about who belongs comes through its design rather than through instruction. Comparing it to a classroom showed me that formal settings have required accommodations while public spaces often treat them as optional. My own bias was that accessibility was mainly a school's responsibility. Watching families navigate a space that was not built with them in mind changed how widely I apply that question.
ES.B Articulate Connections Between Values and OutcomesThe park presents itself as family friendly, but I gathered specific evidence about how the space functioned and connected those features to the actual outcomes for particular families. The gap between the stated value and the lived outcome became the core of my proposal. I concluded that physical accommodations alone are not enough. Awareness, understanding, and patience from staff throughout the park matter just as much, and those are harder to build than a ramp.
ES.C Participate in Informed DebateThe business letter put me in a real disagreement with a real organization. Changes cost money, and the park could reasonably argue that its current accommodations are sufficient. I had to support my position with specific observed evidence rather than general appeals about inclusion, and write for an audience with the authority to say no.

Application: how I will use this in my career

This experience will help me as I work toward becoming a BCBA, because so much of my work happens in community settings. The families I support take their children to parks, stores, and outings, and I now look at those places the way I looked at the park: who the space was built for, and what a family needs in order to take part. Learning that physical accommodations are not enough without awareness and patience from the people in a space will shape how I prepare both children and the adults around them.

Writing the business letter also taught me to back up a request with specific evidence and to write for someone with the authority to say no. I will use that skill when I advocate for a child with a school, an agency, or a business.
