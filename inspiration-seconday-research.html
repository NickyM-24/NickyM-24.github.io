Secondary Research

Topic: Violent Tantrums

Research questions

The research set out to answer four questions: how violent tantrums affect the populations involved, what behavioral interventions (including ABA) exist to address them, what other community innovations (like after-school and at-risk youth programs) are relevant, and what other local solutions and support systems already exist.

What the research found

  • On the agency itself: Bamboo Sunrise Foster Care, based in Henderson, NV, works with at-risk youth and children with behavioral health needs as a Medicaid-based provider, with a focus on communication and inclusivity between children and their support networks.
  • On ABA and behavioral interventions: an ABA therapist typically starts with a functional analysis — identifying the antecedent, or what led up to the tantrum (inability to communicate a need, lack of resources, environmental stressors) — before building a plan to reduce the behavior's frequency and intensity.
  • On residential and community programs: research into at-risk youth programs, case management approaches, and outpatient/IOP programs pointed to the value of multidisciplinary teams and early intervention.
  • On parenting support: Clark County's Triple P (Positive Parenting Program) suite came up repeatedly as an accessible, evidence-based resource for caregivers across different age ranges.

Summary and next steps

Secondary research reinforced that this needed a child-centered, empathetic approach — and it directly shaped the questions used later in the survey and interview guides (what helps a child calm down, what makes things worse, how tantrums are talked about after the fact). It also underscored the importance of recruitment and confidentiality practices given the population involved.

Feedback

Reviewed this work with Mackenzie Dewar (Group Home Supervisor) and Olivia Salinas (Community Supervisor) in September 2023. Olivia suggested talking directly with kids at the agency about how they experience tantrums with different staff members; Mackenzie suggested tracking and comparing how tantrums show up at the group home specifically, which became a thread through the rest of the project.
