Program Outcomes

Identity as an Educator

Bringing professional ethics, reflection, personal values, and beliefs into my practice.

The domain

The Identity as an Educator domain involves the actualization of professional ethics, reflection, personal values, and beliefs into practice. This domain is assessed based on students' abilities to (1) use professional ethics to identify and recover from mistakes in an educational setting; (2) reflect on experiences that take place in culturally diverse educational settings; and (3) embed personal values and beliefs into an articulated vision for their future career.

Arizona State University, Mary Lou Fulton Teachers College. (n.d.). Overview of Ed Studies program domains [Course document].

The work I chose

  • Course: EDU 304: The Bridge Between Coursework, Identity, and Career
  • Work: Empathy and Advocacy Case Study on Dr. Bruce D. Perry

Description

For the Module 5 case study, I chose an educational leader and examined how empathy and advocacy shape their work. I chose Dr. Bruce D. Perry, a child psychiatrist and the creator of the Neurosequential Model of Therapeutics, whose work centers on how trauma shapes the developing brain. Part One was researched and drafted with artificial intelligence and cited. Part Two was my own analysis connecting his empathy and advocacy to my future practice.

I chose Dr. Perry because his work is about the same children I work with and the same question I ask in my sessions: why does a child's behavior make sense once you know what that child has lived through?

Representation: how this work shows my mastery

Writing Part Two made me put my beliefs about children, behavior, and my future practice into words. It also connected my preschool teaching years, my RBT work, and my plans for the future in one place.

The same course began with an Emotional Intelligence self assessment in Module 1, which I consider the most important thing I learned in EDU 304. My strengths were motivation (16) and empathy (15), while self awareness (11), relationship management (8), and self management (7) needed more attention. Knowing that empathy is my strength helped me see why Dr. Perry's work spoke to me, and knowing where I need to grow keeps me honest about the educator I am still becoming.

OutcomeHow this work shows it
IE.A Ethical PerspectivesPerry's work raises a real ethical question for my field: when a child's behavior is severe, do we respond with removal, restraint, and demands for compliance, or do we first ask what happened to them? In my own sessions this means the first minutes go to safety and connection rather than compliance. The full implication is that I sometimes give up a quick win on a target to protect a child's sense of safety, and I have to be able to explain that choice to caregivers and supervisors.
IE.B Connections to ExperienceThis case study connected directly to my work as an RBT with children who have trauma histories, including children in the foster care system. My project data already records what happened before an episode, how long it lasted, and how long recovery took. Perry gave me a reason to read that data as history, not misbehavior.
IE.C ReflectionThe biggest change in my thinking was moving from "what is wrong with this child" to "what happened to this child." As a Montessori preschool teacher, I already knew that patience and understanding mattered most, and I saw that a child's home life shaped what showed up in the classroom. Perry gave me the science behind what that classroom taught me.
IE.D TransferI am taking Perry's idea that empathy can be built into a system and applying it to my role as RBT Trainer. Instead of hoping new technicians develop a trauma informed lens on their own, I can write those steps into onboarding so it is the starting point.
IE.E Core BeliefsOne of my core beliefs is that children are not their behavior. I believe a child's behavior usually comes from not being able to communicate their needs. That belief started in my preschool classroom, where patience and adapting to each child's way of learning worked better than anything else. The case study helped me see that empathy without structure stays personal, while structure without empathy becomes compliance.
IE.F Future SelfPerry's model spread because organizations could be trained in it, and the idea no longer depends on him. That is what I want for my own work: something that keeps working at my site after I move on. My path is RBT to BCBA, then a doctorate, and eventually becoming a trauma therapist in a women's prison, serving a population whose histories almost always begin in childhood.

Application: how I will use this in my career

Dr. Perry's question of "what happened to you" will guide how I work with every client, from children to adults. As I become a BCBA, I plan to build that lens into how I train RBTs and coach caregivers, and later into my work as a trauma therapist in a women's prison, with women whose histories so often begin in childhood. My goal is to leave the people I work with more capable, not more dependent on me.
