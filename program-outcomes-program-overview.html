The Educational Studies program

The Educational Studies program prepares students to support learning in many places, not only in a traditional classroom. It is built on the idea that learning happens everywhere and at every age, so graduates are prepared to work with children, youth, and adults in nonprofits, community organizations, civic groups, and businesses. Students build cultural competence, including self awareness, understanding and valuing others, and knowledge of inequities in society. They also build skills for designing learning experiences for diverse people, fostering equity and inclusion, and communicating, collaborating, leading, advocating, and innovating across professional settings (Arizona State University, Mary Lou Fulton Teachers College, n.d.).

The program is organized around five domains, and each one has its own page on this site:

Education in Society

How education functions in diverse communities.

Act of Learning

Using theory to design learning.

Identity as an Educator

Ethics, reflection, values, and beliefs in practice.

Communication and Collaboration

Professional communication and partnerships.

Leadership, Advocacy, and Innovation

Sustainable solutions grounded in social justice.

Why I chose Educational Studies

I chose the Educational Studies program because I wanted to finish my bachelor's degree in a way that fit the work I was already doing. I taught for five years at a Montessori preschool in Los Angeles with my California teaching credential. When I moved to Nevada, I worked in the school system as a behavior interventionist and thought I wanted to become a special education teacher, but transferring my credential would have required much more money and schooling. I went back to school to finish my bachelor's degree instead, and Educational Studies, with its focus on learning in many different settings, matched my path into applied behavior analysis.

After graduation

With my Educational Studies degree, I plan to continue toward becoming a Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA), serving clients from young children to adults. After that, I plan to earn a doctorate and become a trauma therapist in a women's prison, working with a population whose struggles so often begin in childhood. At every step, I want to be the person who helps someone understand how they learn, the way I had to learn it myself.
