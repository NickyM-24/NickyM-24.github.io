Program Outcomes

Act of Learning

Using educational theory to examine and design learning environments and activities.

The domain

The act of learning domain encompasses the use of educational theory in the examination and design of educational activities and environments. The main skills assessed through this domain are students' abilities to (1) make practical application of educational theories described in the literature; (2) use and apply knowledge from a specific discipline into the design of educational settings; and (3) use the most appropriate tools or systems to design an educational environment or an educational activity.

Arizona State University, Mary Lou Fulton Teachers College. (n.d.). Overview of Ed Studies program domains [Course document].

The work I chose

  • Course: EDU 498: Pro Seminar (internship at A Parentz Touch ABA Services)
  • Work: Designing the pilot for my human centered design project

Description

My project tests trauma informed strategies for children in the foster care system who display severe tantrum behaviors. For the pilot at A Parentz Touch ABA Services, I designed calm down kits, age tiered visual cue cards, a plain language caregiver handout, and three tracking tools: a Session and Tantrum Tracking Log, a Caregiver Readiness and Confidence Survey, and a Strategy Fit by Age Tier chart.

This is the most complete learning environment I have designed. I had to decide what the children needed to learn, what their caregivers needed to learn, where that learning would happen, and how I would know whether any of it was working. Every choice had to be explained to my supervisor, L'Tricia Turner, and several of them changed after her feedback.

Representation: how this work shows my mastery

The learners in this pilot are not only the children. The caregivers and other RBTs have to learn the strategies too, which pushed me to think about how adults learn and to defend my design with reasons instead of preferences.

OutcomeHow this work shows it
AL.A Educational TheoriesTwo theories sit underneath the pilot. The first is trauma informed care, especially Dr. Bruce Perry's sequence of regulate, relate, then reason. A child whose stress response is fully activated cannot learn anything, so every strategy starts with calming and connection before any teaching or direction. The second is applied behavior analysis. The strategies are mostly antecedent strategies, meaning they change what happens before a tantrum instead of only reacting to it. On a community outing, using a lower tone, getting on the child's level, and making sure the children understood the rules before we left the house were major parts of getting them to comply.
AL.B Discipline KnowledgeMy field is behavior analysis, and as an RBT I already knew how to run a plan with a child. What I had to extend was who the learner is. The goal of my project is for caregivers to run the strategies without me, so I had to design for adults who are tired, short on time, and have often already tried things that did not work. My supervisor also asked that children practice regulating in their natural environments, like their homes and community places, which pushed my ABA training into settings I cannot control.
AL.C Learning Environment Tools and SystemsI chose each tool against a few criteria: a caregiver has to be able to use it, it has to fit into a home or a crowded public place, and it has to tell me something I cannot learn another way. The tracking log records the setting for every session because sessions happen at home, at the agency, and in the community. The caregiver survey runs at baseline, at Week 10, and at the end, so I can see change and not just a final number. After a community outing I added a portability requirement, so the calm down kits now include a smaller kit a caregiver can carry.

Application: how I will use this in my career

As a future BCBA, I will be the one writing plans that caregivers and RBTs carry out. Designing this pilot taught me to name the theory behind each strategy, to design for the adults who will actually use it, and to choose tools by whether a caregiver can use them at home or in a crowded public place. I will carry that same approach into my role as RBT Trainer at A Parentz Touch ABA Services, where I want to build trauma informed steps into onboarding so new technicians start with that lens instead of picking it up later.
