Sustainable Revenue

How my project could keep going after my pilot ends in December. A funding strategy gets a solution through launch. A revenue strategy is what gives it long term impact.

  • Pilot budget: cash total stays close to $325
  • Products: the implementation guide and assembled calm down kits
  • Strongest strategy: building the work into RBT onboarding at A Parentz Touch ABA

The short version

My Module 3 budget mostly holds. A portable kit subset for community settings adds roughly $30, and a bilingual volunteer could move translation from cash to in kind, so the two roughly cancel out. What has changed is not the number but the timeframe, because that budget only priced a fifteen week pilot.

Grants were unrealistic for the pilot because grant cycles run slower than my December deadline. For a program meant to continue, that timing problem goes away. Grants should fund expansion rather than basic operation.

My project has two products: the implementation guide, which could be sold or licensed to other agencies, and assembled calm down kits. I would not sell to the families in my pilot. Proceeds from outside sales should subsidize the families inside the program.

The strategy I think is strongest needs the least money. I am an RBT Trainer and already onboard new hires. If these strategies become part of that onboarding, the project becomes part of how the agency already operates, and nothing new has to be raised.

Scaling in stages

StageWhat it means
OneFrom three families to a caseload. Cheapest step, mainly materials for additional families.
TwoFrom me to other staff. Needs a guide another RBT can follow without me, plus training time.
ThreeAgency wide practice. The cost is absorbed into normal operations.
FourBeyond one agency. This is where the guide as a product and grant funding become relevant.
